01 What is the SCOPE Game?

A classroom game about how goods move from a raw-material supplier all the way to a shop — and why that is surprisingly hard to manage.

Two supply chains compete side by side. Each has five stages: a Supplier, a Manufacturer, a Distributor, a Wholesaler, and Retailers. Each student controls one stage and, every week, decides how much stock to order from the stage above. Customers appear only at the shops, but that demand ripples all the way up.

Each student's goal is simple: make the most profit — sell what customers want, without drowning in storage costs or running out. That tension is the whole game.

The swings get bigger as you move up the chain — a real effect called the bullwhip effect, which students feel happen live.

02 The game in 60 seconds

  1. Open the Master screen and pick a few options — or just use the defaults.
  2. Students scan a QR code and each take one role. Empty roles are filled by bots, so it works with any class size.
  3. Press Start. Each week, students see next week's expected demand and place an order upstream.
  4. Orders take one week to arrive. Unfilled demand is a lost sale (no back-orders by default).
  5. After about 20 weeks, the game reveals winners: best student, best chain, best market — with full stats.

03 The supply network

Two chains, five stages each, twelve seats. Students can buy from their own chain (cheaper) or the rival chain (dearer).

04 How one week works

Every week follows the same rhythm. There are two ways to run it — you choose.

One-week deliveryWhat you order this week arrives next week. Plan ahead.
Ship from stock on handYou can only send what you already have at the start of the week.
No back-ordersUnfilled demand is lost forever (unless you turn back-orders on).
Week 0 is a warm-upThe first week builds starting stock. No customer sales yet.
Demand is announcedNext week's total is shown in advance so students can plan.
Two surprisesDemand jumps or drops twice (around weeks 5–12).
Mode B default

Simultaneous · automatic

Everyone orders in one window; deliveries are shared out automatically. Fast — best for large or first-time classes.

Mode A

Two-phase · human sharing

First everyone orders. Then, if a stage is short, that student decides who gets how much. More realistic, more discussion.

05 What students can (and can't) do

Students only play and chat. They never see or change any settings — that is entirely your control.

Every week

Place an order

Decide how much to buy from the stage above — own chain, rival chain, or both.

Mode A only

Share out stock

If short, choose who gets how much. Automatic in Mode B.

If you enable it

Chat

Team chat, market chat, or non-binding negotiation offers.

Students never change settings, pause the timer, or submit twice. Once they submit, they wait for the round to end.

07 Advanced options all off by default

Optional realism settings. Turn any on to change how the game plays; each has a value you can tune.

Fixed order costA flat fee each time a stage orders — rewards fewer, larger orders. $50
Capacity limitMax a stage can ship per week. 1500/wk
Variable lead timeDeliveries take longer than one week. 2 weeks
Back-ordersUnmet demand is carried over, not lost, for a weekly penalty. 0.3/unit/wk
Information sharingEvery stage sees the real customer demand — known to reduce the bullwhip effect. on/off
Bullwhip feedbackEach student sees how much their own orders swing. on/off
Random supply disruptionEach week a stage may suddenly be unable to ship. 10%/wk

08 Run a session — step by step

  1. Open the Master screen on the projector; leave it on the lobby.
  2. (Optional) change any settings. Unsure? Keep the defaults.
  3. Students scan the QR code and pick a role. Empty seats become bots.
  4. Press Start — the game enters week 0 (warm-up).
  5. Each round, point to next week's demand; the round advances when the timer ends or everyone has submitted, or press Advance.
  6. Watch the map and dashboard fill in — great teaching moments.
  7. Near the end, open Compare for market-by-market and chain-vs-chain results.
  8. Open Winners and press Present fullscreen for the big reveal.

10 Mini glossary

Bullwhip effect

Small demand changes cause bigger order swings up the chain.

Service level

The percentage of customer demand you supplied.

Lost sale

Demand you couldn't meet — gone for good (unless back-orders are on).

Holding cost

The cost of stock sitting in storage.

Lead time

The delay between ordering and receiving.

Bot

An automatic player that fills any empty seat.
